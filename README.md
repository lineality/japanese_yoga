# ~Japanese Yoga

The base-idea behind these exercises in "Japanese" yoga (which all-told are my ridiculous invention, not traditional Japanese practice) is that anecdotally it is said that Japanese people's practice of standing up and sitting down frequently on 'tatami mats' (essentially: on the floor, not chairs) is very good daily exercise that keeps Japanese people fit and limber into old age.

The bigger goal was to adapt this type of exercise into something that could exercise the upper-body even more.


So this is broken down into "levels," where each level builds on the previous level.


- level-1 is just sitting down and standing up. (This much likely is a Japanese minimalist form of exercise.)

- Level 2 emphasizes the common intermediate step where weight is supported by the arms, behind your feet, as part of standing up. (It is possible to stand up without using your hands, but that is atypical.) Level two is a sort of upside-down version of 'down on all fours.' (Up on all fours?)

- Loop (Level 1 & 2 themselves can be a kind of loop, sitting down, shifting your weight, and standing up over and over. Almost a kind of mindfullness-of-balance activity.)

- Level 3 is where you pick something up or perhaps just lift one hand (or foot). 

- Level 4 is where you move an object from one side of you to the other. For example: Put a 1-gallon water bottle to either side of you (2 in total). In a loop, move both water bottles from one side to the other side, alternating hands. 

(For whatever reason this is very joint and back-friendly exercise, more so than pullups, pushups, and situps.)
